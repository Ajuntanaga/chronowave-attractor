XII The attractor

Where it all began: pulses on a Lorenz attractor

The origin instrument, rebuilt live. In the DAW experiments, wave pulses were plotted on a Lorenz attractor with Rayleigh and Prandtl values — and the “orbital state jumps” that seeded the whole framework are the trajectory's lobe transitions. Tune ρ and σ, perturb the state, and count the jumps.

M3 stack interval · set in Module XIII

At the default σ = 10, β = 8/3 the fixed points lose stability at ρ ≈ 24.7; on screen the trajectory settles onto one of them below about ρ = 23 — a wavefunction that never pops — and can keep jumping for a long time just under the threshold. Move σ or β and the threshold moves with them. Above it, chaos: consistent, sudden lobe transitions that stabilize without collapsing, exactly the behavior the studio metering showed as long as frequency moves stayed within 100 cents. A perturbation kicks the state the way an intrinsic detune did.
